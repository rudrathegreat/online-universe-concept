# Orbital

A Three.js solar system explorer. Serve this directory with a local HTTP server, for example `npx serve .`, and open the displayed URL. An internet connection is required for Three.js, fonts, and the Sun and Earth textures. The other available planet textures and Saturn's rings are served locally.

Select Solar System from the home screen. Drag to orbit the current destination, scroll to zoom, and use 0–9 to visit the Sun through Pluto. The nine planetary orbits use mean Sun distances in AU and circular paths; their relative distances are to scale. Planet and Sun radii are proportional to their actual sizes, but are enlarged relative to their orbital distances for visibility. The minimum planet size control keeps planets at least 10 screen pixels wide; switch it off to see their relative sizes without that adjustment. Time runs at real-time speed by default: one simulated second per real second. Use the time speed control or the [ and ] keys to decrease or increase the rate, from paused to 1,000,000x; at 1x, planetary motion is very subtle. Press O to toggle orbital lines and L to toggle the destination information panel. Orbits and approximate axial rotations follow this same clock. The orbital animation is illustrative, not an ephemeris. Pluto is included as a dwarf planet.

Planet textures are provided by [Solar System Scope](https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/) (CC BY 4.0), based in part on NASA imagery; Pluto currently uses a solid-color placeholder. These are not direct NASA texture maps. NASA/JPL's [texture map archive](https://maps.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/) is a potential source for a future replacement.
