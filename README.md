# Orbital

A Three.js solar system explorer. Serve this directory with a local HTTP server, for example `npx serve .`, and open the displayed URL. An internet connection is required for Three.js and fonts. The Sun, planet, and Earth cloud textures and Saturn's rings are served locally.

Select Solar System from the home screen. The camera automatically orbits the current destination; press R to toggle auto-rotation. Press Space to pause time and camera rotation together; press it again to restore their previous settings. Use the up and down arrows to zoom and the left and right arrows to rotate around the destination. Drag to orbit manually, scroll to zoom, and use 0–9 to visit the Sun through Pluto. Press Shift+0 through Shift+9 to look at the corresponding object while continuing to orbit your current destination. Press the same shortcut again to return to the normal view; visiting another destination also resets it. The nine orbits use semimajor axes in AU, approximate eccentricities, and inclinations and orientations relative to the ecliptic; their relative distances are to scale. The Sun lies at a focus of each ellipse, and orbital speed varies along the path. Planet and Sun radii use the same physical scale as their orbital distances. The minimum size setting keeps the Sun at least 20 screen pixels wide and planets at least 10 screen pixels wide; press S to toggle it and see their relative sizes without that adjustment. Time runs at real-time speed by default: one simulated second per real second. Use the [ and ] keys to decrease or increase the rate, from paused to 1,000,000x; at 1x, planetary motion is very subtle. Press O to toggle orbital lines and L to toggle the destination information panel. Orbits, approximate axial rotations, and Earth's cloud drift follow this same clock. The clouds complete one extra rotation relative to Earth every 5 simulated days. The solar surface and glow pause and change speed with the simulation clock, up to a maximum of 5x normal speed. Press M after selecting Earth (3) to visit the Moon; pressing M cycles through available moons of the selected planet. Press M after selecting Jupiter (5) to cycle through Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto. The Moon uses a mean radius of 1,737.4 km, a 384,400 km semimajor axis, eccentricity 0.0549, and inclination 5.145? to the ecliptic, with a 27.321661-day sidereal period. Its orbit follows Earth and shares the simulation clock and orbit-line toggle. The minimum size setting also applies to the Moon; press S to see physical sizes. The lunar orbit uses fixed mean elements and an illustrative orientation, so it does not predict the Moon?s actual position or orbital precession. The orbital animation is illustrative, not an ephemeris. Pluto is included as a dwarf planet.

Planet textures are provided by [Solar System Scope](https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/) (CC BY 4.0), based in part on NASA imagery; Pluto currently uses a solid-color placeholder. The Moon uses the local 8k_moon.jpg texture; its source is not documented here. These planet textures are not direct NASA texture maps. NASA/JPL's [texture map archive](https://maps.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/) is a potential source for a future replacement.

Jupiter's Galilean moons use [JPL mean orbital elements](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/sep.html) and [mean radii](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/sep.html). Their semimajor axes are 421,800, 671,100, 1,070,400, and 1,882,700 km; periods are 1.762732, 3.525463, 7.155588, and 16.690440 days; eccentricities are 0.004, 0.009, 0.001, and 0.007; and inclinations are 0.0?, 0.5?, 0.2?, and 0.3? relative to their local Laplace planes, respectively. Their orbits use fixed mean elements and approximate reference-plane orientations, so they are illustrative rather than an ephemeris. All four moons use the supplied local textures and share the Moon's time, minimum-size, and orbit-line controls.

Press M after selecting Saturn (6) to cycle through Enceladus and Titan. Their JPL mean semimajor axes are 238,400 and 1,221,900 km, periods are 1.370218 and 15.945448 days, eccentricities are 0.005 and 0.029, and inclinations to their respective local Laplace planes are 0.0 and 0.3 degrees. Their mean radii are 252.1 and 2,574.76 km. They use the supplied local textures and the same illustrative fixed-orbit model and controls as Jupiter's moons.

Press M after selecting Uranus (7) for Titania, or Pluto (9) for Charon. Titania's JPL mean semimajor axis is 436,298 km, period 8.705869 days, eccentricity 0.002, inclination 0.1 degrees to Uranus's equator, and mean radius 788.9 km. Charon's mean Pluto-relative semimajor axis is 19,600 km, period 6.387222 days, eccentricity 0.000, inclination 0.0 degrees to Pluto's equator, and mean radius 606 km. Their orbital planes use the approximate pole directions of Uranus and Pluto. The simulation places Charon relative to Pluto and does not model Pluto's motion around their shared barycenter. Both moons use supplied local textures and the same illustrative fixed-orbit model and controls as the other moons.

Press M after selecting Neptune (8) to visit Triton. Its JPL mean semimajor axis is 354,800 km, period 5.8792 days, eccentricity 0.000 (rounded), inclination 157.3 degrees to Neptune's equator, and mean radius 1,352.6 km. The inclination gives it a retrograde orbit. Its fixed equatorial reference plane approximates Neptune's pole at J2000. Triton uses the supplied local texture and the same illustrative orbit model and controls as the other moons.

Press M after selecting Mars (4) to cycle through Phobos and Deimos. Their irregular shapes are approximate and use the supplied equirectangular surface maps. The Deimos TIFF is converted losslessly to PNG for browser compatibility. Their mean orbital distances are 9,376 and 23,463 km, with periods of 0.31891 and 1.26244 Earth days. Their orbits use an approximate Mars-equatorial reference plane and the same illustrative orbit model and controls as the other moons.
