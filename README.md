# Orbital

A Three.js solar system explorer. Serve this directory with a local HTTP server, for example `npx serve .`, and open the displayed URL. An internet connection is required for Three.js and fonts. The Sun, planet, and Earth cloud textures and Saturn's rings are served locally.

Select Solar System from the home screen. The camera automatically orbits the current destination; press R to toggle auto-rotation. Drag to orbit manually, scroll to zoom, and use 0–9 to visit the Sun through Pluto. The nine orbits use semimajor axes in AU, approximate eccentricities, and inclinations and orientations relative to the ecliptic; their relative distances are to scale. The Sun lies at a focus of each ellipse, and orbital speed varies along the path. Planet and Sun radii use the same physical scale as their orbital distances. The minimum size setting keeps the Sun at least 20 screen pixels wide and planets at least 10 screen pixels wide; press S to toggle it and see their relative sizes without that adjustment. Time runs at real-time speed by default: one simulated second per real second. Use the [ and ] keys to decrease or increase the rate, from paused to 1,000,000x; at 1x, planetary motion is very subtle. Press O to toggle orbital lines and L to toggle the destination information panel. Orbits, approximate axial rotations, and Earth's cloud drift follow this same clock. The clouds complete one extra rotation relative to Earth every 5 simulated days. The solar surface and glow pause and change speed with the simulation clock, up to a maximum of 5x normal speed. Press M after selecting Earth (3) to visit the Moon; pressing M cycles through available moons of the selected planet. The Moon is currently the only moon in the model. The Moon uses a mean radius of 1,737.4 km, a 384,400 km semimajor axis, eccentricity 0.0549, and inclination 5.145? to the ecliptic, with a 27.321661-day sidereal period. Its orbit follows Earth and shares the simulation clock and orbit-line toggle. The minimum size setting also applies to the Moon; press S to see physical sizes. The lunar orbit uses fixed mean elements and an illustrative orientation, so it does not predict the Moon?s actual position or orbital precession. The orbital animation is illustrative, not an ephemeris. Pluto is included as a dwarf planet.

Planet textures are provided by [Solar System Scope](https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/) (CC BY 4.0), based in part on NASA imagery; Pluto currently uses a solid-color placeholder. The Moon uses the local 8k_moon.jpg texture; its source is not documented here. These planet textures are not direct NASA texture maps. NASA/JPL's [texture map archive](https://maps.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/) is a potential source for a future replacement.
